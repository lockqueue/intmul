\section{Reference implementation}\label{app:impl}\label{sec:impl}

To complement the written proofs and the Lean~4 formalization of Section~\ref{sec:lean}, we wrote an executable
Python~3.13 reference implementation of every algorithmic component in this paper. Its role is to make the data layouts,
rounding rules, error budgets, and finite-network certificates concrete and testable at small sizes; it is not a fast
multiplier and does not simulate multitape Turing-machine head steps; some address permutations inside the engines are realized as index maps, and built-in integer multiplication is used for inner products unless the hierarchy mode is selected.

\paragraph{Exact-arithmetic design and implemented components.} No floating-point arithmetic occurs on any authoritative
data path. Every intermediate and stored value is represented in exact integer or rational arithmetic, and every
rounding step is an explicit integer operation. On the dyadic grids of the fractional-shift bridge
(Section~\ref{sec:bridge}) this operation is rounding to nearest, whereas on the disk-valued records of the butterfly
layers and middle transforms (Sections~\ref{sec:walsh}--\ref{sec:direct-fft} and Appendix~\ref{app:parameter-transfer})
it is componentwise truncation toward zero. All transcendental tables (sinc-Gaussian spreading taps, cyclic
deconvolution coefficients, and roots of unity) are computed from certified rational interval enclosures (with Ziv
precision refinement), guaranteeing faithful rounding to the target grid.

The package implements the complete pipeline in modular form:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The $d$-dimensional fractional-shift bridge with negative half-bin modulation, overlap-add spreading in axis order
  $d,\dots,2$, chronological cyclic deconvolution $B_2^{-1},\dots,B_d^{-1}$, and two-call root packing and recovery.
\item The direct complex tensor FFT of Section~\ref{sec:direct-fft}, including the three-layer selective $H_0$ gadget,
  slice-batched $\CNOT$ flips, two-involution positional permutations (Section~\ref{sec:walsh}), and combined per-record
  twiddles, as well as the Bluestein middle transform and normalized $\Rr_r$-convolution of
  Appendix~\ref{app:bluestein}.
\item The recursive power-width and arbitrary-width chunk interchanges, the packed selected-bit addition with its
  eight-shift program and deterministic radix-sort repair on the exceptional set, and the recursive $C^{\otimes e}$
  phase-frame engine of Appendix~\ref{app:parameter-transfer}.
\item A non-recursive inner-multiplier hierarchy so that end-to-end runs can route every packed and word-scale integer
  product above $64$ bits through the internal multiplier rather than built-in big-integer multiplication.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Test-scale execution and asymptotic caveat.} As noted in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}, the sub-$n\log n$
regime activates only at astronomically large input lengths ($d\ge2$ already requires $k\ge2^{15}$ at $\eps=1/15$, and
the guard allocation $\Delta=\lceil C_0d^{14}\rceil$ involves $C_0\approx2.27\times10^{139}$), while the $h=100$ triple
networks have $m=10^6$ and $W_c\approx1.87\times10^{21}$ roles. Consequently, the unforced theorem schedule falls back
to a two-dimensional $O(N\log N)$ fractional-shift multiplier with negative half-bin carrier (the $d=2$ specialization
of Section~\ref{sec:bridge}; cf.~\cite{MS} for the real-kernel two-dimensional multiplier) at every feasible input
size. To exercise the sub-$n\log n$ machinery end to end, test policies
force small dimensions and transform shapes ($d\in\{2,\dots,10\}$, lengths up to $R=4096$, chunk widths $K\in\{1,2\}$).
They then run the recursive interchange and butterfly-layer engines on small test-scale network fixtures ($W=3$,
$m\in\{2,3\}$) with guards compiled from the active fixture's depth recurrence. These small fixtures satisfy all
structural, scratch-restoration, frame, and endpoint identities of Definition~\ref{def:fixed-network-premises}, exercising
every recursive, basis-change, and exceptional-repair path. They do, however, have $s>Wm$ and therefore no rank saving.

\paragraph{What the test suites verify.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Bit-exact products and component equivalence.} Across more than $2{,}000$ unit and integration tests
  (and, separately, one full-cascade hierarchy run on each of the Walsh and Bluestein middle transforms), every integer product
  returned by the multiplier is verified bit for bit against Python's independent integer product. Component suites
  likewise verify bit-identical agreement between the network-driven interchange and butterfly-layer engines and direct
  dense/reference operators, and check runtime invariants (exact zero-bit halvings, guarded-width bounds, disk
  containment, scratch restoration, and hole-key bijection in the sorting repair).
\item \emph{Measured errors against proved bounds.} Across $71$ transform configurations spanning dimensions
  $d\in\{2,\dots,5\}$ and lengths up to $R=1024$, the measured final rounded error is at most $2^{-2.51}\cdot2^{-p}$
  (within the final rounding term $(\sqrt2/8)\,2^{-p}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:bridge-precision}). On the $15$ non-leaf runs
  of the two-dimensional fallback path ($17$ configurations in total, including $2$ base-FFT leaves), pre-rounding
  errors lie at least $12.9$ bits below the nominal node certificate. That certificate is nominal because the fallback
  rounds twist roots and leaf twiddles to nearest rather than into the unit disk. Pre-rounding errors lie at least $21.5$ bits
  below the proved analytic budget on the $d$-dimensional bridge with a direct reference middle transform ($24$
  configurations), and at least $23.9$ bits below on the Walsh and Bluestein middle-transform routes ($30$
  configurations). Direct tests of the Walsh middle transform against multiprecision reference DFTs also confirm its
  $12\ell\cdot2^{-q}$ bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-fft}).
\item \emph{Exact symbolic network certificates.} For the $h=100$ triple networks of~\cite[\S3]{OAI26}, a symbolic
  certifier exploits $(S_h)^3$ orbit symmetry and exact rational cell calculus in exact arithmetic without constructing
  $10^6\times10^6$ matrices. In about two seconds per network, it verifies the closed-form role counts $W_b,W_c$, edge-rank sums
  $s_b,s_c$, endpoint identities, guard constants ($\nu_{\mathrm{net}}=11$ and the $140$-digit integer $C_0$), and
  relative deficits $\eta_b,\eta_c$. An exact rational interval series for $\ln m$ produces the width-$2^{-80}$ enclosure
  rounding outward to $13.815510557964\le\ln10^6\le13.815510557965$ (Table~\ref{app:cert-closed} and
  Remark~\ref{rem:deficit-margins}), independently confirming the certified exponents $\tau=1-2^{-40}$ and
  $\sigma=1-2^{-42}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:deficits}. (For the bit network's odd prime radix $q_{\mathrm{rad}}$,
  whose existence follows by finite avoidance in Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer-shear}, a symbolic grammar
  certificate and brute-force elimination over all $96{,}172$ ordered one-factor primitive matrices at
  $h\in\{7,8,10,11,12\}$ find denominator and pivot-numerator prime support $\{2,3\}$. This provides computational
  evidence for $q_{\mathrm{rad}}=5$; no theorem of this paper relies on this value.)
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Availability.} The exact rational parameter-verification script \texttt{tools/h5\_rows\_check.py} is included
in the source bundle of this paper. The companion software artifact~\cite{Code26} comprises the full Python~3.13
exact-arithmetic reference package described in this appendix, while the separate directory~\cite{CGMP26}
(\texttt{companion/impl/}) contains the standalone C/GMP/MPFR implementation (\texttt{intmul\_fastdft.c}), benchmark
runner, and raw logs (\texttt{bench/\allowbreak BENCHMARK.md}, \texttt{bench/\allowbreak logs/}) of the two-dimensional
$O(n\log n)$ multiplier of~\cite{MS} (Sections~\ref{sec:intro-verification} and~\ref{sec:disc-limitations}). Together
with the Lean~4 formalization~\cite{Lean26} (Section~\ref{sec:lean}), both software directories are available in our
\href{https://github.com/lockqueue/intmul}{github repository}.
