\begin{abstract}
We give a simpler algorithm that multiplies two $n$-bit integers in $O\bigl(n(\log n)^{1-\kappa}\bigr)$ bit operations on a multitape Turing machine, with $\kappa=2^{-46}$, and we formally verify this bound in Lean~4. The only previously known algorithm below $n\log n$ obtains its advantage from two small fixed linear networks but runs them inside the full architecture of the Harvey--van der Hoeven $n\log n$ algorithm. We keep the two networks and replace everything around them by a construction on a single power-of-two grid: one Fourier transform, preceded by a smoothing step that is later undone to working precision. This also improves the exponent from $2^{-182}$ to $2^{-46}$. Every step of the algorithm is implemented in exact arithmetic and tested bit for bit. The Lean development proves the complexity bound and independently verifies the certificates of the two networks, assuming nothing beyond Lean's standard axioms.
\end{abstract}
